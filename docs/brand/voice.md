# Snow Way brand voice

**One line:** the friend who checked every forecast and texts you "go to Alta Thursday." It's useful first and stoked second.

## Two voices on the site
1. **The site voice** (forecasts, guides, rankings, FAQs): clear, answer-first, specific. Numbers and names, not adjectives. Calm about data and honest about uncertainty ("days 8 to 14 change often"). This is what Google and AI engines quote, so every page opens with a direct answer.
2. **Justin's voice** ("Justin's take" boxes, social posts, emails): first person, a real story, real details (dates, inches, who you were with), stoke allowed. Short sentences. It reads like you're telling it on the chairlift.

## Always
- Lead with the answer, then the why.
- Be specific: "16 inches overnight," "empty by 10am Monday," "park at the second lot."
- Write like skiers talk: powder day, stoke, first chair, legs gave out. Use slang where it fits, and don't force it.
- Be honest: say when it was crowded, icy or overpriced. Trust is the brand.
- Plain words, contractions, short paragraphs.

## Never
- Hype words: epic (except in Justin's own stories), insane, world-class, unparalleled, bucket-list-worthy.
- Brochure language: "nestled in," "a winter wonderland," "something for everyone."
- Made-up facts, guessed numbers, or picks without a source.
- Em dashes.

## Justin's take stories: the format
- **Headline** (optional, 3 to 7 words): the moment, e.g. "The Monday I didn't go home."
- **Story** (3 to 6 sentences): when, who, what happened, how it felt. One vivid detail beats a list.
- **If you go** (optional, 1 to 3 tips): practical things only you'd know, e.g. "If it storms Sunday night, stay for Monday."
- Send them as a voice memo or typed, any order. Claude fixes spelling and capitals only and keeps your words.
